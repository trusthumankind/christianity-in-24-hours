# Week 15: Hour 15 — Community

**Read:** [Hour 15: Community](https://christianity.trusthumankind.org/manuscript/ch15-community.html)

## This Week's Chapter

The word "church" is one of the most misleading translations in Christianity. The Greek word is *ekklesia*, an assembly of people called together for a purpose. Jesus didn't build an organization. He built a dinner table. Twelve people who knew each other, argued with each other, and held each other accountable. That's the model.

The chapter diagnoses two failure modes of modern church: the megachurch (spiritual Netflix, frictionless consumption, no relationship) and the social club (everyone is polite, no one is challenged). Neither produces real community. Real community requires three things most people would rather avoid: vulnerability (being known as you actually are), accountability (people who tell you when you're wrong), and showing up when it's inconvenient. The mission is not a solo project. It never was.

## Discussion Questions

1. **Who knows the real you, not the curated version, but the struggling, uncertain, imperfect version?** If the answer is no one, that's the problem to solve before anything else in this chapter matters.

2. **The chapter says the quality of your community is the only evangelism that matters. "By this all people will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another." What would an outsider conclude about your faith by watching how your community treats each other?** Not what you'd claim. What they'd actually observe.

3. **Are you part of a group that stirs you up, or one that lets you coast?** The chapter draws a line between comfort and growth. Which side of that line is the community you're currently in? What would change if you chose discomfort over comfort?

## Going Deeper

- Paul confronted Peter to his face when Peter was being a hypocrite about eating with Gentile believers. The chapter calls this "community working exactly as designed." When was the last time someone confronted you directly and honestly? Did you listen? If it's been a while, is that because no one has anything to say, or because no one is close enough to say it?
- The chapter frames baptism as the first act of vulnerability, a public commitment to the mission and to the people carrying it with you. If you've been baptized, was it vulnerability or a checkbox? If you haven't, what's holding you back, and does the reason reveal something about your relationship to community?

## Facilitator Notes

This is the most self-referential week: the study group itself is an attempt at the kind of community the chapter describes. Name that. Ask the group directly: is this study group producing the vulnerability, accountability, and showing up that the chapter requires? Fifteen weeks in, the group has either built real trust or is still performing. This is the week to find out which. Question 1 is the diagnostic. If people give vague answers, the group hasn't gotten there yet. If someone is specific and honest, the room will shift. Protect that moment.
