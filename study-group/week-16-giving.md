# Week 16: Hour 16 — Giving

**Read:** [Hour 16: Giving](https://christianity.trusthumankind.org/manuscript/ch16-giving.html)

## This Week's Chapter

The 10% tithe was a tax for a theocratic society where the church was the government. You don't live in that society. The New Testament doesn't prescribe a percentage. What it says is far more demanding: give as you've decided in your heart, not reluctantly or under compulsion. Paul isn't letting you off the hook. He's raising the bar.

But the chapter pushes past money entirely. Are you hoarding your time? Your comfort? Your safety? The widow who drops two copper coins gives more than the billionaire who writes a seven-figure check, because giving that costs nothing changes nothing. The early church benchmark wasn't "everyone gave 10%." It was "there was not a needy person among them." The chapter argues that lending exists because we stopped sharing, and interest is the price of a missing community.

## Discussion Questions

1. **If someone audited your bank statements, what would they say you actually worship?** Not what you'd claim in a group setting. What the evidence shows. Where your money goes reveals where your heart is, and Jesus said exactly that.

2. **Are you giving in a way that costs you something, or in a way that's painless enough to ignore?** The chapter distinguishes between writing a check (easy) and examining everything you're holding onto (hard). Which one describes your current approach?

3. **The early church standard was "not a needy person among them." What would it take for this group to meet that standard?** Not theoretically. Practically. What would each of you need to give, share, or change?

## Going Deeper

- The chapter argues that capitalism is scaffolding, not the endpoint. Useful for a stage, but not the destination. The destination is Acts 2, a community where no one needs to borrow because no one is left without. Do you believe that's achievable? If not, what does that say about your faith in the mission?
- Ananias and Sapphira didn't die because they kept some money. They died because they lied about it. Where in your life are you pretending to give more than you actually are? Not just money, but time, attention, sacrifice?

## Facilitator Notes

Money is the topic people least want to discuss honestly in a group setting. The discomfort is the point. Question 1 lands hardest when people answer honestly. If the discussion stays theoretical ("society should..."), pull it back to the personal. Question 3 is deliberately provocative. It asks the group to apply the early church standard to themselves, right now. This might feel premature at Week 16, or it might be exactly the right time. Read the room, but don't let the group off the hook. The chapter's point is that giving is the concrete expression of love: if you love your neighbor as yourself (Week 13), your bank statement should reflect it.
